# Features

**See everything at a glance**

- **Panel icon:** Talpy peeking up through the panel's edge, in the state color: orange and popping up, with a red badge counting the chats that need you; blue and glancing around while Claude works; green and smiling when it's your turn; grey and asleep when nothing runs. The 5-hour usage of your default account sits next to it (see [Accounts](usage.md#accounts)).
- **The big Talpy** at the top of the popup sums it all up. Its stage glows orange (gently pulsing) when a chat needs you, blue while chats work, green when it's your turn, and turns into a starry night once everything is done. Its headline says what matters most, e.g. *2 chats need you*: click it to go there. Under it, a line says what the working chats do right now (*fix parser ▸ Edit: parser.py +12 −3*), each new step as it comes, taking turns between the chats at work: click it for that chat's pane. Double-click its stage a little away from Talpy and there's a cookie for him, now and then of another flavour: he spots it, runs after it as you drag it around (and jumps for it when you hold it up; let go and it floats down to the floor, where you can pick it up again), eats it, tastes it his way, says thanks now and then, and digs in right there. Nothing is counted: a cookie is just a treat (Plasma and VS Code).
- **Changes show the moment they happen:** Talpy Den watches the files Claude Code keeps for its chats, so a chat that starts, ends, starts working, asks for something or finishes its turn shows at once (also when you answer or interrupt it in its terminal), with its pet, notification and sound. **Live mode** (a setting) shows each step of each chat as it happens too: every tool call in its row, its pane and the line under the big Talpy. Between changes Talpy Den checks far less often than before, so it costs less while your chats sit idle. (Not on GNOME yet.)
- **One section per Claude account** (profiles logged into the same account are merged; the default account comes first): plan usage with reset countdowns (from the status line; for chats in Claude's VS Code panel, which runs none, Claude Code fetches it while they work: see [How it works](how-it-works.md)), tokens for today and the last 7 days, and every chat (terminal, background job or VS Code) with its state, how long it's been in it, project folder, model and reasoning effort (e.g. *Opus 5.5 · max*, and *ultracode* when it's on) and context fill, plus what a waiting chat asks for (e.g. *approve Bash: rm -rf build/*).
- **Focus on what needs you:** the filter button in the popup's header (**Focus**) shows only what needs you, as full rows (their questions, Allow and Deny, Go on), the chats that finished a turn you haven't looked at yet (until you open that chat), one line for each chat at work with what it does now, and how the rest stands. **Show everything** goes back to every account, chat, pet and usage bar.
- **What each chat is doing, without switching to it:** under each chat, a line says what it's doing right now (`▸ Bash: npm test`), what it needs, Claude's recap of it (when you come back to a chat after a while, Claude Code writes one: *You asked …; Next step: …*) or what Claude said last, with a bar for its task list (*2/5*). Click the line to read your last prompt, Claude's reply and the chat's tasks. A chat in plan mode, auto mode, accept edits or don't ask says so next to its name; one that bypasses permissions says so in red.
- **Chats a usage limit stopped** say so in red, with when the limit resets (a background chat too, which Claude marks as blocked); their pet runs out of steam, and the notification says the chat hit the usage limit, not that it finished. Once it has reset, a notification says which chats can go on. Meanwhile **Continue on personal** moves the chat to another account with room left (see [Sharing chats](sharing.md)), or **Go on at 10:41** has it go on by itself once the limit resets: in the background, in the permission mode it had and on the model and reasoning effort it last ran on (the answer says which, e.g. *Opus 5.5 · xhigh*), telling Claude the limit has reset (an idle Claude that still has it open quits first). **Keep going** beside it does that after every later limit too, until the chat is done (with no reset to go by, out of usage credits say, it tries again after 2, 5, 15, 30 and 60 minutes, then gives up and says so); a chat at work gets the same from its ↻ button. In **Recent chats**, click a chat's **Limit** tag (once) or **↻** (keep going) for the same. Stopping a chat that keeps going (■) asks whether to leave Keep going on (**Stop**: it goes on after the next limit) or to turn it off too (**Stop and turn Keep going off**).
- **Where your usage is heading:** each usage bar shows where you'd be when the window resets at your pace (the faded part), a mark where an even pace would be by now, and a warning when you'd run out first (*At this pace: full in 1h 20m, 40m before it resets*).
- **A usage cap** (a setting, off until you set it): once an account's 5-hour or weekly window reaches the share you pick (say 90%), the account's chats at work on this computer are stopped, once in that window (in tmux and VS Code's Claude the turn is interrupted and the chat stays open). Queued tasks wait until the window resets, and chats that keep going go on then. Chats you start or answer yourself afterwards aren't stopped again (Plasma and VS Code).
- **Your last days in numbers:** click an account's token line for its last 14 days: tokens per day as bars, chats and prompts, your busiest folders and the models used. Each chat's unfolded box ends with its own recap (*3 prompts · worked 54m · 313 tool calls (Bash 265, Read 25) · 100.6M tokens · $37.06 at API prices*).
- **Your days with Claude:** the popup's **Recap** tab shows a day, your last 7 days or everything since your first chat (**Day | Week | Overall**; step back a day or a week at a time). Claude Code deletes chats after 30 days, but the Recap keeps their numbers, so **Overall** keeps growing (a setting turns that off, asking first when the history holds chats Claude has deleted, since their numbers then go for good; **Delete Saved History…** starts it again). For the 7 days, from the big picture down to the money: how long you were at it, how long Claude worked, your prompts, its tokens and what the same work would cost at Claude's API prices, against the week before, with a trend line of the last five weeks under each; what Claude did (tool calls, commands, edits with the files and lines they changed, subagents, web searches, compactions); a timeline with a row per day that shows when you were at it, when Claude worked (darker where several chats worked at once) and when it waited for your answer (point at a day to see what went on then; click a day for its chats), red marks where a usage limit stopped a chat; how full your plan's windows are now and how they filled up over the same days; the day chat by chat; the projects Claude worked in (click one to open it in the **Projects** tab) and the chats it worked on most; the API prices by model, with what the cache saved; and what would use less of your plan: the tips of Claude Code's own `/usage` (*Start fresh when you switch tasks*, *Save max effort for hard tasks*, *Compact before you step away*, …), each with the share of the week's usage it touches and a command to copy, and what coming back to chats whose cache had expired cost. For all your accounts or one, in US dollars or your currency. It's read from the chats on this computer and the SSH hosts shown, so it uses no tokens, and only while the tab is open (the first time takes a few seconds; after that only what's new is read). On Mondays a notification sums up the last 7 days (*Your week with Claude*), counting the SSH hosts shown as the Recap does, and opens the Recap (a setting turns it off).
- **The permissions you gave, in one place:** the popup's **Permissions** tab lists everything that lets chats go without asking you, here and on the SSH hosts shown: rules, modes and loosened checks in your settings, your administrator's and every folder's `.claude` settings, approved MCP servers, and the folders you trusted. **Revoke…** changes that one entry in its file (on the host for a host's). Under it, the permission prompts you answered in the last 7 days, with the rules that would have let the most of them through (**Allow…** writes one, **Undo** takes it back; a setting leaves them out). Read from your settings and the transcripts, so it uses no tokens.
- **A pet for every chat:** Talpy lives in a burrow, and each account's chats get one on a little stage under its name, in the same order as the list (the chats that need you first, the others in the order they started), wearing the chat's name and a calm version of its color. The chat's row in the list gets a bar in that color. A pet acts its chat out, half out of its burrow: it works (types, thinks, hunts bugs, checks tests, juggles subagents), pops up waving or holding up a sign when the chat needs you, plays when it's your turn, sleeps once the chat is done and looks sad if it failed. The lamp on its head glows in the chat's state color. Click a pet to go to its chat (its terminal or VS Code tab).

**Act on your chats**

- **Chat with any chat from the panel:** click a chat and it opens beside the popup: the conversation as it goes, drawn the way Claude's terminal draws it: a dot before each reply and tool call, Claude's replies rendered (headings, lists, code blocks with a **Copy** link, tables), each tool call on one line with what came of it under it (*● Bash(pytest) ⎿ 3 passed*, the dot green or red; click it for what it ran and got), updating while it works. **A−** / **A+** (or Ctrl+wheel) set its text size. Below it, a box to reply (Enter sends, Shift+Enter starts a new line, **Ctrl+V** pastes a picture, shown under the box): **Send**, or **Send when this turn ends** (Talpy Den keeps it and sends it once the turn is over; on an account at its usage limit, once the limit resets). Your message goes the way the chat can take it: typed into its tmux pane; into the input of its VS Code tab (press Enter there); or, for a background chat or one that isn't open, the chat goes on with it in the background (`claude --bg --resume`, the same chat; a background chat that's running stops first). A terminal like Konsole takes no text from outside, so there you choose: send it, and Claude in the terminal quits and the chat goes on in the background, or copy it and open its tab. An SSH host's chats work the same, over the connection Talpy Den already has. A notification takes a reply too.
- **Dictate a reply:** the pane's microphone (**Dictate**) records what you say (up to five minutes, with `pw-record`, `parecord` or `arecord`) and writes it into the reply box, with speech to text that runs on this computer: what you say never leaves it, and the recording is deleted once it's written down. The first click offers to set it up, with no sudo: faster-whisper from pypi.org in a Python environment of its own and its base speech model from huggingface.co (about 600 MB), in `~/.local/share/talpy-den/whisper` (with whisper.cpp installed, that's used instead and only a model is downloaded). The settings pick the language, download, delete or pick other models (up to 1.5 GB each), and **Remove faster-whisper…** deletes it all again.
- **Answer permission prompts from the panel:** a chat that asks for a permission shows what it asks, the command or the file with its change, with **Allow once**, **Always allow** and **Deny** (with a note for Claude), in Claude's own order, in its row, its pane and its notification. That takes the opt-in setting *Answer permission prompts from Talpy Den*: it adds a hook to each Claude profile's `settings.json` (those in *Extra config dirs* too, and on the SSH hosts shown) that hands each request to Talpy Den. Claude still asks in the chat too; whichever you answer first counts. **Always allow** saves the rule Claude suggests, as its own *don't ask again* does. Chats in tmux can be answered without the hook, with their dialog's keys, unless the prompt may be about another call than the one shown (a subagent's, or one of several calls Claude made at once): those you answer in the chat. **Keyboard answers** (a setting, off until you turn it on) answer the chat that has waited longest for a permission from anywhere: **Meta+Alt+Y** allows it once (a plan: approves it), **Meta+Alt+N** denies it (a plan: keeps planning), and a note on screen says what was answered; pick other keys in System Settings → Shortcuts → Talpy Den. In VS Code they're **Talpy Den: Allow the Oldest Prompt** and **Deny the Oldest Prompt** (Ctrl+Alt+Shift+Y and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+N). Both take the hook.
- **Answer Claude's questions from the panel:** when Claude asks you a multiple-choice question, its row and its pane show each question with its options (choose one, or any for a question that takes several) and a field to answer in your own words, then **Send answers** or **Decline…** (with a note for Claude). Its notification has a button for each option when it's one question with one answer, then **Other…** (the pane, ready to type); with more questions, **Answer…** opens the pane. Your answers reach Claude as they would from its own dialog. This also takes *Answer permission prompts from Talpy Den* (without it, the question shows, to answer in the chat).
- **Review what a chat did:** the pane's **Review** tab lists the files it changed with their added and removed lines, against how they were before the chat (from Claude's own backups), each with its diff and **Undo**; **Undo all** puts them all back (a file the chat made is deleted for good, and the question says so). **Commit these files…** commits only this chat's files (yours stay as they are, and files git ignores are left out) with a message made from its name and Claude's recap, that you edit first. Finished chats that changed files say **To review** until you **Mark reviewed** (or until they change files again), with whether tests ran and passed after their last edit (**Tests ✓**, **Tests ✗**, **Not tested**).
- **Jump to a chat** with the window button beside it or its pet (or by clicking its name, when the setting *A click on a chat opens it beside the popup* is off): Konsole switches to the right tab (or tmux to its pane), VS Code opens the conversation, and a background job is attached in a terminal. Finished chats reopen with `claude --resume`.
- **Start a new chat** with the speech bubble with a plus next to an account: pick one of its recent project folders, or **Choose folder…** to pick any folder in the folder dialog, and a terminal opens with Claude in it. Or switch the **Terminal | VS Code** switch at the top of the list to VS Code, and the chat starts in VS Code's Claude Code panel instead (the switch is kept for next time). Without the `claude` command, **Terminal** is greyed out; without VS Code, **VS Code** is (the tooltip says why).
- **Start it with a task, also in the background:** write what Claude should do in the field above the folders, then click a folder (Ctrl+Enter: the first one). In a terminal the task is sent right away, in VS Code it's typed into the new conversation for you to send. With **In the background** no window opens: Claude's background service runs the chat (`claude --bg`), and it's listed right away, so you see when it needs you. That works in folders Claude trusts already (it can't ask there), so never in your home folder. Pick the model, the reasoning effort and the permission mode (manual, accept edits, plan, auto, …) it starts with above the folders; the next chat starts with the same. The field grows with what you write, and **Ctrl+V** pastes a picture from the clipboard (a screenshot, say) for Claude to look at, with a thumbnail under the field (it needs `wl-paste` or `xclip`). For an SSH host's chat, the box asks to copy it to the host first.
- **Wait for the usage limit, or start later:** with an account at its limit, **After the limit resets** queues a background task (in a recent folder, or **Choose folder…**): it starts by itself a minute after the limit resets. **Later:** starts it on a day and at a time you pick instead (today or in the next six days). Queued tasks (and chats that go on by themselves) are listed under their account, to start now or cancel (a task or a reply with your own text in it asks first, since the text goes with it).
- **Two chats changing the same files** get a red **⚠ Same files** tag (and a notification): they can overwrite each other's work.
- **Memories:** each project in the **Projects** tab lists what Claude remembers there: read one, change it (✎), hide it (×) or delete it (the trash button). Pointing at one shows its folder, *(not there any more)* when that folder was moved or deleted. Each account has its own `CLAUDE.md` as its last line (your instructions for every chat on it): read it, or change it (✎).
- **Find a chat:** the magnifier at the top looks through all your chats, here and on the SSH hosts shown: every one with all the words you type in its name, its folder or one of your prompts, newest first. Click one to go to it, or to reopen it. It finds project folders (click one for a new chat there), plans and memories with those words too. Each kind, and each computer, folds under its heading (the chats show at first); **Unfold all** shows everything.
- **Plans:** a chat that wrote a plan in plan mode gets a **Plan ↗** tag that opens it rendered (in Okular where it's installed, else in your browser; a setting can pick VS Code's preview or your own app), and a **✎** that opens its Markdown file to change it, in your text editor (an SSH host's in VS Code, over Remote-SSH). Once you changed a plan, **↩ Send to Claude** sends your changes (and notes you wrote into it) to the chat that made it, which revises the plan in plan mode. Every plan is listed in its project (the folder of the chat that made it) in the **Projects** tab, and the ones you starred under their account's **★ Starred plans**: open one, edit it (✎), send your changes (↩), **carry it out** in a new chat (the plan is the chat's first message, with your own notes, in a terminal, VS Code or the background), go back to the chat that made it, share it with your other accounts, hide it (×) or delete it (the trash button).
- **Stop or interrupt** any chat that's running, from the list (■; it asks first and keeps the conversation).
- **Hide or delete:** the × on a finished chat, a plan, a memory or a project (in the **Projects** tab) hides it from the list (nothing is deleted), and the right-click menu shows each kind again. The trash button beside it deletes a finished chat (also under **Recent chats**), a plan or a memory: it asks first, then moves it to the Trash (see below).
- **Reach every button with the keyboard, or a finger:** a row's buttons (Stop, Star, Share, Keep going, Hide, Delete…) show while its name or one of its buttons has the keyboard's focus, and Tab reaches the names of chats, recent chats, plans, memories and projects (Enter opens one, as a click does). On a touch screen, press and hold a row to show its buttons (Plasma; VS Code shows them on focus too, and GNOME while a chat's name or one of its buttons has the focus).
- **See what a chat changed:** **±3** next to a chat counts the files it changed. Click it to compare them in VS Code, side by side: each file as it was before the chat first changed it, and as it is now. The box under a chat also compares only its last turn.
- **A shortcut to the chat that needs you:** give Talpy Den a key (its settings → Keyboard Shortcuts). It brings up the chat that has waited longest for you, then one that finished while you weren't looking; press it again for the next one. With none, it opens the popup.
- **Recent chats** per account, to reopen older conversations: in a terminal or in VS Code, with the same switch at the top of the list. They show a page at a time (as many as the *Recent chats* setting), newest first: **Show N more** under them shows the next page, the title says how many of all show (*Recent chats (10 of 23)*), and folded again the list starts over. The chats and plans you starred are under **★ Starred chats** and **★ Starred plans**, however old, a page at a time too.
- **Projects:** the popup's **Projects** tab has everything by project (a chat's git repository, else its folder), newest first, each folded, with when Claude last worked there, a dot for each of its open chats (in its state's color), a button for a new chat there and × to hide it. Open one for its open chats, Claude's last recap of it, and its chats, plans and memories, each a page at a time and with the same buttons as elsewhere; when a project has chats on more than one account, each line says which. A folder that's gone says *(not there any more)*. How long Claude worked there and what it cost are in the Recap, whose *Where Claude worked* opens a project here.
- **Notifications** when a chat needs you or finishes a long turn, with sounds from your sound theme (quiet during Do Not Disturb). Click one to open its chat beside the popup (or, with that setting off, its terminal or VS Code tab); **Open chat** goes to the chat itself. **While you're away** (the screen locked for 5 minutes or more) they wait: when you're back, one notification sums up what finished, failed or needs you. Meanwhile, chats that need you can go to your phone with KDE Connect (off until you turn it on).

**Beyond this computer**

- **SSH hosts:** pick hosts from your `~/.ssh/config`, and their Claude accounts, current chats, usage and recent chats join the dashboard. Nothing is installed on the host.
- **Several profiles and accounts:** every `~/.claude` and `~/.claude-*` config folder is found automatically, including linked ones such as `~/.claude` → `~/.claude-work`.
- **Add, log in, log out and remove accounts, and pick the default one** in Talpy Den's settings (**Accounts**), here and on your SSH hosts, with no extra tool: a new account is a `~/.claude-NAME` folder that Claude's own login signs in, in a terminal. The default account comes first everywhere, its usage is in the panel, and VS Code's Claude and plain `claude` run on it (see [Accounts](usage.md#accounts)). An account that isn't logged in shows **Log in…** in the popup.
- **Share chats between accounts:** continue a finished chat on another account, e.g. when one hits its limit (see [Sharing chats](sharing.md)). A shared chat is listed once, where it runs, with a pointer from the other accounts.

- **SSH hosts:** the SSH hosts button at the top lists the hosts in your `~/.ssh/config` (with `Include`s). Tick one and its Claude accounts show up below your own, like a second machine on the same dashboard:
  - its chats count in the panel icon and at the top of the popup, and send the same "needs input" / "finished" notifications (with the host's name)
  - click a chat (or a recent one) to bring it up. A background or finished chat opens in a local terminal running `ssh -t <host> claude attach …` / `claude --resume …` in the right folder. A chat running in **VS Code connected to the host (Remote-SSH)** shows in that VS Code window (the one titled `[SSH: <host>]`), which opens on the host if it isn't open yet. A chat running in a terminal here (`ssh <host>`, then `claude`) brings up that terminal tab.
  - the new-chat bubble starts a new chat there, in a terminal or in VS Code connected to it: on an account other than the one VS Code's Claude runs on there, VS Code's Claude on that host switches to it first (in the host's VS Code server settings, `~/.vscode-server/data/Machine/settings.json`) and stays on it; ■ and × stop its background and idle chats, or hide finished ones (the eye button next to the host's name shows them again), and the trash button deletes one to the host's own Trash
  - with two or more Claude accounts on the host, its chats can be shared between them (see [Sharing chats](sharing.md))
  - checked every 30 seconds (configurable) while it's ticked; the ✕ next to the host's name hides it again
  - a host that wants a password (or a passphrase, a one-time code, or a new host key confirmed) shows **Log in…** (Plasma): it opens a terminal where `ssh` itself asks you, so Talpy Den never sees or keeps what you type. The checks then go through the connection that login opened, until 30 minutes after their last check, until you log out, or until you click **Disconnect** next to the host's name (terminals already open over it go on, and it closes with the last of them). With key-based login (your key in the agent, `ssh-add`) none of this is needed. The host needs `python3`. Nothing is installed there: the status script is sent over the connection each time. It keeps its caches in the host's `~/.cache/talpy-den`, like here. One connection per host stays open for a few minutes, so checks through a jump host are quick.
- **Subagents:** a chat that started subagents shows an **Agents 2/3** tag (finished/total), outlined while any are running. Click it for a tree of its agents, nested agents included: each one's type and the model it runs on (e.g. *Explore · Haiku 4.5*), its task, running/done/stopped, and what it is doing right now (e.g. `Bash: pytest -x`, `Edit: parser.py`, or the first line of its final answer).
  - **Click an agent** for what it was asked to do, its tool calls and tokens so far, its last steps and, once it's done, the report it handed back.
  - **Open the whole run** shows everything it did as a Markdown file, in the app you use for those: its task, each step (what it wrote, every tool call with its input and the start of the result, messages the chat sent it while it ran) and its report. On an SSH host it's fetched over your ssh connection. Claude Code itself has no way to open a subagent from outside (its terminal shows an agent's run only when you pick it there with the keys, and a subagent isn't a chat you can resume), so this is for reading.
- **Shared chats:** a chat shared across profiles (one transcript hard-linked into each profile) gets a **⇄ Shared** tag. Its tooltip lists the profiles and, when known, which one has it open. It's listed once, under the account it runs on, or, once it's finished, the account it was used on last. Every other account it's shared with shows a short line pointing there (e.g. *⇄ my chat · on personal · Working*), and clicking it does what clicking the chat's name does.
  - When Claude forks a shared chat into a chat of its own (e.g. when a background chat takes it over), that copy gets a **⇄ Shared copy** tag: its new messages are only on that account. If the shared chat continued in the copy, the copy is listed instead of the chat, and the other accounts point to it (*copy on personal*).
  - A shared chat's tokens appear in every profile it's shared with, since a transcript doesn't record which account paid; the token tooltip shows how much that is.
  - **Share from Talpy Den:** hover a finished chat (or a recent one) and press its share button: **Continue on work** (shares it if needed and opens it there with `claude --resume`, the quick way on when one account hits its limit), **Share with all accounts**, or **Keep only on personal…** (asks first). Nothing is done to a chat while Claude has it open somewhere. See [Sharing chats](sharing.md).
- **Pets:**
  - a chat's pet stays the same pet, with the same color, as long as the chat is in the list, even while it finishes and runs again. The pets stand in the list's order (a chat that starts needing you moves to the left), so they dig over to new spots when a chat comes or goes; a new chat's pet usually lines up on the right. A new chat's pet digs up out of the ground; when the chat leaves the list (you hide it, its terminal closes, or it gets old), the pet waves and digs away, also once you open the popup if that happened while it was closed. Something that happened to a chat while its pet was out of sight is acted out when you look, if it was in the last minute. Up to 5 pets per account (configurable); with more chats, the ones that need you get a pet first, and "+N" counts the rest.
  - the big Talpy takes the news that isn't about one chat's pet, such as limit resets. It picks what to do at random from what fits the moment, so it's never the same twice: while Claude works it types, thinks, gets ideas, hunts bugs with a magnifier or reads the docs; at ≥ 90% of the 5-hour limit it's mind-blown now and then, and at 100% it runs out of steam. When things are quiet it digs over to wherever your mouse is, sneezes itself out of its burrow, blows bubbles, floats off with a balloon, plays peekaboo, listens to music and greets you on holidays.
  - click Talpy to pet it (click quickly to tickle it), double-click to make it dance, drag it out of its burrow (it hangs on to the rim for a second, leaning towards your hand, then pops out into it) and drop it: it gets dizzy from high up, then digs back in where it landed
  - notification icons show Talpy with a sign (needs input), cheering (finished), sad (failed) or with an empty battery (a usage limit stopped the chat)
  - they only move while the popup is open
  - when your desktop asks for less motion (Plasma's animation speed set to *Instant*, GNOME's *Reduce Animation*, or your system's reduced motion in VS Code), they keep still: the big Talpy doesn't dig about the stage, a pet whose spot moved is just there, a pet pulled out of its burrow comes out at once and doesn't swing in your hand, a cookie is eaten where it lies, and the light and the stars don't pulse or twinkle; it follows the setting as it changes
- **Jumping, in detail:** chats in a terminal: Konsole switches to the tab running it (or tmux to its pane) and the window comes to the front. chats in VS Code: the conversation opens in the VS Code window of that project. Background jobs: the terminal already showing the job comes to the front; if there is none, a new terminal runs `claude attach <id>` in the job's folder. A background job shown in a terminal (`claude attach`, or picked in Claude's agent view) gets a **Terminal** tag (**Tmux**, **VS Code terminal**), whatever the terminal: the terminal counts for the chat it was opened for, by its id; where the terminal names its tab (Konsole, Yakuake, tmux) and the title is another listed chat's name, the agent view switched to that chat, and of several chats with that name only the one it was opened for counts. A finished background job is reopened the same way; if it ran in a folder Claude doesn't trust yet (always so for your home folder), Claude first asks whether you trust it, with "No, exit" preselected, and the terminal says so before the question comes up.
- **New chats in VS Code:** VS Code opens the folder (or brings up the window that has it), then the Claude extension opens a new conversation there. Which account it runs on is up to VS Code, not Talpy Den: its `claudeCode.environmentVariables` setting (`CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`), else VS Code's own environment, else `~/.claude`. So **VS Code** in the switch only works on that account; on the others it's greyed out and says which account VS Code is on, and their new chats open in a terminal. For an SSH host, VS Code connects to it (Remote-SSH) and the chat starts once it's connected. The same switch sits at the top of an account's **Recent chats**: in VS Code, a recent chat reopens in the folder it last ran in, in a new tab of the Claude Code panel.
- **Terminal or VS Code, whichever is there:** **Terminal** is greyed out where the `claude` command isn't found (on PATH, in `~/.local/bin`, npm's or nvm's folders, or on your login shell's PATH, which is asked at most once an hour), and chats open in VS Code there. **VS Code** is greyed out where VS Code isn't installed. On an SSH host, Terminal depends on the host's `claude`.
- **Hiding** a finished chat, a plan, a memory or a project (×) only hides it in Talpy Den: nothing is deleted, and you can still resume the conversation. A hidden chat comes back by itself if it runs again. Right-click → *Show Hidden Chats Again (N)*, *Show Hidden Plans Again (N)*, *Show Hidden Memories Again (N)* or *Show Hidden Projects Again (N)* brings that kind back (each shows only while something of its kind is hidden); an SSH host's come back with the eye button next to its name. A hidden project's chats, plans and memories stay in their own lists. This computer and each SSH host keep their own.
- **Deleting** (the trash button) asks first, saying what goes and on which accounts (*Delete “Fix the build” on work and personal? It goes to the Trash with its subagents and checkpoints; the Recap keeps its numbers.*), then moves it to the Trash, where you can restore it from: a chat's transcript with its subagents and checkpoints, on every account it's shared with; a plan's file, on every account it's on; a memory's file, and its line in that folder's `MEMORY.md` (the rest of the file stays as it was), so Claude stops reading it in new chats there. An SSH host's goes to that host's Trash. A chat open in Claude isn't deleted (*It's open on work. Exit it there first.*). The Recap keeps a deleted chat's numbers; its star, Keep going and queued go-ons go with it. An account's `CLAUDE.md` and projects can't be deleted here (a project can be hidden).
- **Stopping** a background chat runs `claude stop <id>`: the conversation is kept and `claude attach <id>` resumes it. A chat that is *working* is interrupted mid-step, and anything it started in the background stops too.
  - A **chat in a terminal** can be stopped whatever it's doing: Claude gets the signal it gets when its terminal closes and shuts down cleanly. A working one's current step is cut off, and a waiting one quits without your answer. Its terminal stays open, and the chat moves to **Recent chats**, from where it reopens.
  - A **chat in VS Code's Claude tab** is stopped the way the tab's own stop button does it: the tab says *Interrupted*, keeps the chat and goes on with your next message (which starts Claude again, a second or two). Claude gets SIGINT there, its clean exit; SIGTERM would put an error in the tab.
  - A **working chat in tmux** can be **interrupted** instead: Ctrl+C in its pane, as if you pressed it. Like the stop button in VS Code, that stops the turn and its subagents, background ones too, and keeps the chat open (a draft you typed stays). Esc would leave background subagents running. Other terminals take no keys from outside, so their chats can only be stopped.
  - The confirmation says which of these happens. For a chat that keeps going after usage limits it says so too, and offers **Stop** (Keep going stays on, so it goes on after the next limit) and **Stop and turn Keep going off**. Talpy Den looks again before stopping: an idle chat that got back to work since you asked isn't touched. Stopping ends every subagent of the chat, since they run inside its Claude; commands it leaves behind are ended too, and a Claude that hasn't quit after 10 seconds is killed.
- **What a chat changed:** Claude backs up a file before a chat first changes it (in `file-history/`, the backups its undo uses). The comparison puts each file as it was then next to the file now; with the [Talpy Den extension for VS Code](../vscode/) 1.8 or newer, all of them open in one view, else each in a tab of its own (at most 25). *Last turn* compares with the files as they were when you sent your last prompt. A file the chat edited with no backup on record (e.g. in a subagent, or outside its folder) is compared with the file as the chat's first Edit or Write of it found it, else with git's last commit; *Last turn* leaves such files out. Changes made through shell commands (`sed`, scripts) aren't counted, since Claude doesn't back those up.
- **The Recap tab, in detail:** *You* is the time between your prompts (and your answers to Claude's questions and permission prompts) and Claude's replies, where that gap is under 15 minutes, plus a minute for each prompt you write: time away doesn't count. *Claude worked* adds up Claude Code's own count of each turn (its *Worked for …*), which leaves out the time a turn waited for you; a stretch of half an hour with nothing written in a turn (the computer asleep, a usage limit) doesn't count either. Chats working at the same time each count; *on the clock*, in the tooltip, counts that time once. The cost is each reply's tokens at the API list prices (cache reads and 5-minute or 1-hour cache writes at their own prices), as Claude Code prices its `/usage`; a reply in two transcripts (a forked chat) counts once. The tips use the rules, thresholds and weights of Claude Code's `/usage` (*What's contributing to your limits usage?*), and add how long Claude waited for your answers and how often a usage limit stopped you. A *cold resume* is a reply more than an hour after the chat's last one that writes 20k tokens or more of its context to the cache again (on a plan the cache keeps a chat's context an hour); its cost is what that write cost more than reading the same tokens. The plan windows are this computer's accounts', from the usage Talpy Den saw (the 5-hour and weekly bars). On a day the clocks change (23 or 25 hours), the Week view draws that day's times by its own clock, under the same 0, 6, 12, 18 and 24 and quarter lines as the other days. A chat shared between accounts counts on each account that has it. What was read is kept in `~/.cache/talpy-den/week-3.json`.
- **Rules for permission prompts:** the Permissions tab lists the prompts you allowed in the last 7 days in a chat on this computer and the rule that would have let each through: a folder of another project to read (`permissions.additionalDirectories`), edits in the chat's own folder (`permissions.defaultMode` `acceptEdits`), a read-only command (`Bash(git status *)`, `Bash(grep *)`, …; not `rg`, `tree` or `git branch`, `diff`, `log` and `show`, whose options can run programs, write files or delete branches) or a site's pages (`WebFetch(domain:…)`). Only those: never a prompt Claude flagged as unsafe, one you refused, a command with pipes, redirects or `&&`, nor anything in a hidden config folder of your home (`~/.ssh`, `~/.config`, …), outside your home or in a temporary folder. **Allow…** says what it changes, and on **Allow** the rule goes into the `.claude/settings.local.json` of the folder the chats started in (Claude reads it for chats started there; git doesn't share it), keeping what's in the file; your user settings never change. **Undo** takes back a rule it added, as it was (the file goes again if Talpy Den made it). A rule your settings have already (the folder's, or the `settings.json` of the accounts whose chats asked; not Don't ask mode, which refuses instead of asking) shows *Allowed now*. The setting *Suggest rules for the permission prompts you answered* leaves the rules out, with their **Allow…** buttons: the tab then only counts the prompts.
- **Permissions you gave:** the Permissions tab lists everything that lets chats go without asking you, here and on the SSH hosts shown (each as listed there): in the settings files (each account's `settings.json`, the `.claude/settings.local.json` and `.claude/settings.json` of every folder Claude ran in, and your administrator's `/etc/claude-code/managed-settings.json`), the `permissions.allow` rules, `permissions.additionalDirectories`, a `permissions.defaultMode` that skips prompts (accept edits, auto, bypass permissions), approved `.mcp.json` servers, and the settings that loosen a check (bypass or auto mode without their warning, fetching pages without the site check, commands without asking in the sandbox, remote control, unattended remote tools, channels, chats uploaded by themselves); and in each account's `.claude.json`, the folders you trusted (those that still exist) and what was approved in them. The rules Claude saved when you answered *don't ask again* are among them. **Revoke…** says what it changes in which file; **Revoke** then changes that entry only (on a host, there), keeping the rest of the file: a rule or setting goes, a trusted folder is marked untrusted (Claude asks again before it works there). A rule the tab's **Allow** added goes as **Undo** would take it. Not changed here: a folder's `.claude/settings.json` (shared through git: *In git*) and your administrator's (*Administrator's*).
- **The Recap in your currency:** Claude's API prices are in US dollars. Pick another currency in the settings (**Recap amounts in**), and the amounts are turned at the European Central Bank's euro reference rate of the day: the Recap tab downloads it from `ecb.europa.eu` at most every 6 hours, only while it's open, and keeps the last one for when you're offline. The tab's last line says the rate and its day.
- **Usage forecast:** your pace is the last hour's in the 5-hour window, and the window's so far in the weekly ones (or while there's no hour on record yet). Talpy Den notes each window's usage as it changes, in `~/.cache/talpy-den/usage.json`.
- **Your phone (Plasma):** with *Phone* on, once the screen has been locked as long as set, a chat that needs you is sent to your phone with `kdeconnect-cli --ping-msg` (its name, what it asks and its folder), and if you like a chat that finishes a turn too. The phone gets it while KDE Connect reaches it (the same network). Pair the phone in System Settings → KDE Connect first.
- **While you're away:** Talpy Den asks every 10 s whether the screen is locked. Once it has been for 5 minutes (a setting), chats that finish or need you don't notify one by one; when you unlock, one notification (if notifications are on) and a card at the top of the popup say what happened since you left. Clicking the notification goes to the chat that needs you.
- **Notifications:** when a chat needs you (a permission prompt, a question, or a background job that stopped to ask you something; closed again once you answer), and when a chat finishes a turn that took at least 30 seconds (configurable, or off), or a usage limit (or an expired login) stops it. Sounds: one each for "needs input", "finished" and "failed", from your sound theme, a sound file of your own, or none. They go through PipeWire's notification stream, so the system's notification volume applies. When several chats change at once, only the most urgent sound plays.
